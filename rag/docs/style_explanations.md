# Portfolio Style Explanations

Balanced style combines quality, growth, valuation, momentum, risk, and income without a single dominant tilt. It is the default choice when the user wants a diversified portfolio but does not express a strong factor preference.

Growth style favors companies with stronger revenue growth, earnings growth, analyst upside, and positive price momentum. It can improve upside potential but may increase valuation risk and drawdown sensitivity.

Value style favors lower valuation multiples such as forward P/E, price-to-sales, and EV/EBITDA. It can improve margin of safety but may underperform during momentum-led markets.

Dividend style favors income, payout sustainability, quality, and stability. It is useful for users who want cash yield and lower volatility, but it may reduce exposure to high-growth companies.

Quality style favors profitability, return on equity, free cash flow, and balance-sheet strength. It is often suitable for medium- and long-term investors who want durable compounders.

Defensive style favors lower beta, stronger liquidity, resilient sectors, and quality. It is suitable when the user wants less downside sensitivity, though it may lag during aggressive bull markets.
